Pioneering transparency initiative

Why private equity is in focus
Objectifying transformation performance
Added value for investment practice

The transformation of the economy is necessary – and already underway. Transparency regarding transformation performance has so far not kept pace. Now Transforming Performance shows for the first time, based on robust data, how private equity and venture capital firms position themselves on transformation and how to recognise firms with pronounced transformation competence.

Why private equity
is in focus

The development of new technologies and their scaling, like the transformation of business models, typically take place outside public markets. Private equity and venture capital actively support established companies in their transformation or scale the technologies of tomorrow – and so play a decisive part in shaping the successful companies of the future.

Objectifying
transformation performance

Until now, standardised data has been lacking on how and to what extent private equity and venture capital firms reflect transformation in their structures, decision logic and portfolios, and what return effects they experience. Transforming Performance closes this gap and enables a fair comparison among peers.

Added value
for investment practice

Based on compact checklists, investors can assess the status quo, recognise good transformation practice and direct capital specifically to where transformation ambition and competence come together. Private equity and venture capital firms can locate their own position and, if interested, derive further steps.

Eight findings at a glance

The report provides in-depth insights and illustrates the data with a wide range of figures.
The executive summary condenses the results into eight findings.

  1. Relevance
    and anchoring

    Nine out of ten firms take transformation into account in investment decisions. Following the chain from strategy to financial incentives, the field shrinks with every step: only nine percent anchor it without gaps.

  2. Signal
    and substance

    Seven out of ten firms are members of at least one transformation or sustainability initiative. Yet anchoring remains patchy: only 38 percent of PRI signatories have concrete transformation plans and 22 percent a net-zero target.

  3. Size
    and segment

    Larger firms address transformation more often, but not more deeply. The market segment makes a difference in depth, not in whether a firm addresses transformation systematically at all. Transformation competence therefore cannot be read from these structural variables.

  4. Economics
    and allocation

    Four out of five firms hold climate solution assets, mostly with portfolio shares well below 50 percent. The reasons are economic: industry trends and sector attractiveness, at 83 and 80 percent, rank far ahead of regulation and investor pressure.

  5. Performance effects
    and dynamics

    More than four out of ten firms report positive performance contributions, only two percent negative ones. A positive assessment of the performance contribution and the depth of anchoring correlate clearly.

  6. Development
    and maturity

    Four maturity clusters show the market’s stages of development: 14 percent reach the leading group, 16 percent are still at the beginning. Reaching the leading group requires, beyond the minimum score, among other things linking transformation targets to financial incentives.

  7. Investors
    and their partners

    Investor requirements work – above all where accountability is additionally formally defined. Telling transformation substance from mere signal takes specific questions, but not many. The checklists in the report provide the essential ones.

  8. The bigger
    picture

    Transformation takes place where a firm recognises economic opportunities and links strategic objectives with processes, accountability and incentives. The internal coupling of these elements is a key measure of firms’ transformation competence.

Questions about the project

What does transformation mean in this study?

+

It refers to the transformation of companies to secure their long-term ability to succeed under changed geopolitical, technological, ecological and demographic conditions. The study focuses on two areas, climate and nature, which are economically material in terms of adaptation and mitigation and at the same time offer the necessary data availability.

Why did GPs participate?

+

Participants have a so far unique opportunity for independent benchmarking. Anyone who wants to shape future-proof companies will find in Transforming Performance a useful basis for internal assessment, strategy development and credible communication with investors.

What data does the project analyze?

+

Transforming Performance collected reliable information on firms’ transformation position, including strategic relevance assessment, strategies, information on the investment process, governance and incentive systems, as well as concrete portfolio facts. Both qualitative and quantitative aspects were captured.

What happens next?

+

The research and strategy report (in German) and the executive summary will be available for download here from 3 November. As a next step, we will discuss the findings and their implications for action with investment firms and investors in a range of formats. If you would like to be kept informed about upcoming activities or would like a direct exchange, please get in touch.
